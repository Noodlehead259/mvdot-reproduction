1. All 80 joint-training epochs are present in the log, and a checkpoint is reported saved after each epoch.

4. Sample-matching loss Lsm moves from 0.4635 at epoch 1 to about 1.13–1.15 for most later epochs. It does not trend down like Lrec and Lmm; its magnitude alone should not be interpreted as failure without checking the exact objective and sign/normalization implementation.

6. The reported sparse matching marginal errors are tiny (epoch-end maximum row error ~1.94e-10 and column error ~1.81e-10). This validates numerical marginal satisfaction for the sparse solver, not semantic matching quality.
7. Evaluation metrics are suspiciously discontinuous. Most epochs are around ACC 32–40%, NMI 0.24–0.34 and ARI 0.14–0.20, but 12 epochs abruptly jump to ACC 63–69%, NMI 0.58–0.63 and ARI 0.46–0.54, then frequently fall back on the next epoch while losses barely change. Treat these peaks as potential evaluation/matching instability until the same checkpoint is evaluated repeatedly with a frozen candidate graph and deterministic inference.
8. Do not select epoch 57 as the final model solely because it has the highest displayed scores; first repeat inference for the same saved checkpoint with fixed graph candidates/random seed, and inspect label-to-index alignment and final fused prediction generation.
9. This log is from a paper-oriented approximate implementation and a constructed NoisyMNIST variant. It is not sufficient to claim an exact reproduction of the authors' code or to compare directly against the paper's benchmark table.

INTERPRETATION AND NEXT CHECKS
------------------------------
A. The representation/reconstruction and transport components appear to settle: Lrec steadily improves and Lmm becomes small. Lsm remains around 1.15, so monitor its exact definition and contribution rather than optimizing the total loss blindly.
B. The most urgent issue is evaluation instability. For example, epoch 34 has ACC 32.55%, NMI 0.2479, ARI 0.1529; epoch 35 jumps to 63.30%, 0.5806, 0.4566; epoch 36 returns to 32.55%, 0.2509, 0.1514. Similar jumps recur at epochs 42–43, 57, 59–60, 66, 70, 72, 74–75 and 78. Such alternating scores are not credible evidence of smooth learning progress by themselves.
C. Recommended diagnostic before another long run: load one saved checkpoint (start with epoch 57 and a lower-scoring nearby checkpoint such as epoch 58), run evaluation 3–5 times without updating weights, log random seeds, freeze the sparse graph/candidate set, and compare predictions. If results differ, the variance comes from inference/matching randomness or nondeterminism rather than weight learning across those repeated evaluations.
D. Also inspect the prediction-generation step: verify that clustering labels are produced from the same defined consensus representation every time; Hungarian matching should be applied only for ACC reporting and must not change predictions used for NMI or ARI.
E. After deterministic evaluation is established, choose the checkpoint based on stable repeated metrics, not a single spike. Then evaluate the aligned-rate protocol separately if the goal is to reproduce the paper's experiments.
